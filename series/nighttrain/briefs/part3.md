## Summary
June hangs up the phone that whispered your name and warns you never to answer. She reveals she missed her stop in 1987 because a figure wearing her mother's coat waited on the platform, and she has ridden the train ever since. You glance at the rules again and recognize the handwriting as your own — the loops, the crossed sevens. In the black window your reflection keeps reading after you look up, then blinks first; you look away just in time. The route map flickers and the next stop changes to your name. The train slows, June begs you not to get off, and through the opening doors your mother waves, smiling too wide.
