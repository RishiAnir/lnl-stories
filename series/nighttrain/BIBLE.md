# Don't Miss Your Stop: story bible

## Premise
You fall asleep on the last train home and wake up somewhere that is still a train but not the right one. Your phone says 2:14 AM and won't change. The windows are black. The passengers sit perfectly still, all facing forward, reading newspapers they never turn. In the seat pocket is a folded sheet of rules. A kind older woman named June says she has been riding a long time and tells you which rules are lies. The question for all five parts: do you trust the paper or the person? The answer (Part 5): the rules were true all along. You wrote them yourself, the last time you were here, and June crosses out exactly the ones that would get you off the train. Rules horror, second person: the viewer is "you". Creepy, never gory. Nothing is ever explained outright.

## The rules (handwritten on a folded sheet, numbered)
1. Do not make eye contact with the other passengers.
2. When the conductor asks for your ticket, smile. Do not speak.
3. If the lights go out, keep your eyes closed until the doors chime.
4. Never go into the last car.
5. If someone calls your name, it isn't them.
6. Your stop is the one that shows your name on the map. Get off, no matter what is on the platform.
7. Do not fall asleep again.
8. If your reflection blinks before you do, look away.
9. Count the passengers at every stop. If there is one more than before, do not let them sit next to you.
June crosses out rule 2 and rule 6 in Parts 2 and 3. Both are true. Rule 9 is the quiet clue about June: in Part 1 you count nine passengers; after the conductor passes there are ten, and the tenth (June) sits next to you. Nobody points this out until Part 5.

## Characters
- **You**: early 20s, coming home from a late shift. Yellow over-ear headphones around your neck (signature), olive green backpack, gray hoodie, white sneakers. Never named on screen; the voice that calls your name in Part 2 says it, but the narration never does.
- **June**: late 60s. Silver bun held with a pencil, long camel coat over pale blue nurse's scrubs, knitting a scarf that is far too long and trails across the floor (signature). Warm, chatty, calls you "sweetheart". Her ticket is yellowed and dated 1987. She has been riding ever since. One in, one out: she needs someone to take her seat before she can leave, and she knows the rules better than anyone. Never says this outright; the clues are fair (she sends the man in red to the last car; her ticket; she flinches when you smile at the conductor).
- **The Conductor**: very tall and thin, navy uniform with brass buttons, cap pulled low so his eyes are always in shadow, a wide fixed smile. White gloves, a silver ticket punch that clicks. Moves only when you aren't looking straight at him. Speaks only one phrase: "Ticket, please."
- **The man in the red tracksuit** (Parts 1 and 2): 30s, loud, red tracksuit, gold chain, keeps trying the door handles. Thinks it's a prank. June tells him the last car is the way out. He goes, the lights go out, and when they come back his seat is empty and his phone is still ringing on it.
- **The passengers**: gray coats, gray faces, identical newspapers dated the same day. They turn their heads all together, slowly, when a rule is broken. Never speak.

## Sets
- trainCar: a subway or commuter car interior seen down its length. Two rows of seats, grab poles and hanging handles, ads above the windows, black windows with your reflection, flickering fluorescent tubes, the connecting door at the far end.
- routeMap: the lit route map above the doors. The stop names are wrong (Halloway, Ninefield, Last Light, Ash Street), and in Part 3 one of them changes into your name.
- rulesSheet: close-up of the folded lined paper, handwritten rules, June's pen strike-throughs.
- phoneScreen: 2:14 AM, no signal, a call coming in (Part 2).
- gangway: the rattling gap between cars, rubber bellows, the door window into the last car (only darkness and one hanging handle swinging).
- platform: a station seen through the doors. Tiled wall with the station name, one flickering light, and someone waiting who shouldn't be there.
- ticket: close-up of a paper ticket with punched holes; June's 1987 ticket.
Lighting: CAR (cold green-white fluorescent, flickering), DARK (lights out: only the phone screen, the route map glow and the platform light), PLATFORM (warm, wrong).

## Outline
1. **Wake up**: you wake on the train. Every passenger is facing your way and they all look away at once. Phone: 2:14 AM, no signal. The map shows stops you've never heard of. You find the rules in the seat pocket. At the first stop you count the passengers like rule nine says: nine. The man in red tries the doors and laughs it off. Midway twist: "Ticket, please." The conductor is at your seat. You smile and say nothing, like rule two says. He punches your ticket and moves on, and every passenger turns to look at you. Cliffhanger: you count again. Ten. June sits down next to you, knitting, and whispers, "Rule two is a lie, sweetheart. You just smiled at him."
2. **The last car**: June crosses out rule two. She says she's been riding a long time and the rules were written to keep people on. She tells the man in red the last car is the way out. He goes. The lights go out; you keep your eyes shut (rule three) and hear the punch click right by your ear. The doors chime, the lights come back, and his seat is empty with his phone still ringing on it. Midway twist: every newspaper in the car has the same date. Cliffhanger: your own phone rings. No signal, but it rings. It's your mom's name on the screen, and when you answer, she says your name. (Rule five.)
3. **The handwriting**: June takes the phone from you and hangs up. "Never answer." You look at the rules again and notice the handwriting: it's yours. In the black window your reflection is still reading the page after you've looked up, then it blinks first (rule eight); you look away just in time. The loops, the crossed sevens, all of it. June goes quiet, then crosses out rule six too: "Your name on the map is how he gets you off at the wrong stop." Midway twist: June's ticket falls out of her knitting bag. It's dated 1987. She's been on the train for decades. Cliffhanger: the map above the doors flickers, and the next stop changes to your name.
4. **Your stop**: the train slows. Through the doors, the platform is your street, and your mom is standing there waving. Rule six says get off no matter what. June holds your wrist: "That isn't her." You stay. The doors close, and as the train pulls away the woman on the platform stops waving and starts running alongside. Midway twist: the conductor comes back and punches your ticket a second time; June flinches. Two holes. Cliffhanger: June says she'll find another way out and walks into the last car. A moment later, from the last car, June calls your name.
5. **Don't miss your stop**: you put it together. June sent the man in red to the last car. June crossed out the two rules that would get you off. Your own handwriting wrote the list for you, from last time. Rule nine: she was the tenth passenger, the one who wasn't there before. You follow every rule exactly: eyes down, smile at the conductor (third punch), eyes shut when the lights die and June's voice is right beside you. The map shows your name again. You get off, no matter what's on the platform (this time it's June, smiling, blocking the doors). You wake up on the real train at your real stop, 2:14 AM, with a folded blank sheet of paper in your hand. Final image: you start writing. "1. Do not make eye contact..."

## Story rules
- Open every part on its hook, the strangest thing, in the first sentence.
- Something happens every few seconds. A rule shows up on screen as the handwritten line when it matters, never as a list read out loud. At most one or two rules per part are quoted.
- The characters talk: June is warm and chatty with a sharp edge, the man in red is loud, the conductor only ever says "Ticket, please."
- A twist midway through each part, and a cliffhanger at the end. Never spoil the June reveal before Part 5, but keep every clue fair.
- Times shown on the phone, never read as numbers more than once.
