# Don't Miss Your Stop: story bible

## Premise
You fall asleep on the last train home and wake up somewhere that is still a train but not the right one. Your phone says 2:14 AM and won't change. The windows are black. The passengers sit perfectly still, all facing forward, reading newspapers they never turn. June, an older woman knitting across the aisle, gives you a meek, sorry smile, like she's sad you're here, and hands you a folded sheet of rules. She's trapped too: she missed her stop in 1987 and has been riding ever since, and she's kind to you because she knows exactly what's coming. The rules are all true. The mystery is who wrote them, and the answer (Part 5) is you: you've been on this train before, you got out by following them exactly, and you left the list behind because you knew you'd fall asleep on this line again. Rules horror, second person: the viewer is "you". Creepy, never gory. Nothing is ever explained outright.

## The rules (handwritten on a folded sheet, numbered)
1. Do not make eye contact with the other passengers.
2. When the conductor asks for your ticket, smile. Do not speak.
3. If the lights go out, keep your eyes closed until the doors chime.
4. Never go into the last car.
5. If someone calls your name, it isn't them.
6. Your stop is the one that shows your name on the map. Get off, no matter what is on the platform.
7. Do not fall asleep again.
8. If your reflection blinks before you do, look away.
9. If the passengers start copying you, freeze. Do not move again until they go back to their newspapers.
Every rule is true. Nobody crosses anything out. The danger is in breaking them: the man in red breaks rule four, June once broke rule six, and in Part 4 you break rule six too, because June begs you to.

## Characters
- **You**: early 20s, coming home from a late shift. Yellow over-ear headphones around your neck (signature), olive green backpack, gray hoodie, white sneakers. Never named on screen; the voice that calls your name in Part 2 says it, but the narration never does.
- **June**: late 60s. Silver bun held with a pencil, long camel coat over pale blue nurse's scrubs, knitting a scarf that is far too long and trails across the floor (signature). Gentle, tired, calls you "sweetheart", always a meek, sorry smile. She doesn't want to be here either. In 1987 her stop came, and what was on the platform scared her so badly she stayed in her seat, and she's been riding ever since. Her ticket is so full of punch holes it's almost lace. She helps you, and she's honest, but her fear is the trap: in Part 4 she begs you not to get off, the same mistake she made.
- **The Conductor**: very tall and thin, navy uniform with brass buttons, cap pulled low so his eyes are always in shadow, a wide fixed smile. White gloves, a silver ticket punch that clicks. Every stop you miss, he punches your ticket once more. Moves only when you aren't looking straight at him. Speaks only one phrase: "Ticket, please."
- **The man in the red tracksuit** (Part 1): 30s, loud, red tracksuit, gold chain, films everything on his phone, sure it's a prank show. June saves him from the conductor once; then he mocks the passengers, they copy him, he won't freeze, and he's gone. His face ends up on a newspaper's front page.
- **The passengers**: gray coats, gray faces, identical newspapers dated the same day. They turn their heads all together, slowly, when a rule is broken. Never speak.

## Sets
- trainCar: a subway or commuter car interior seen down its length. Two rows of seats, grab poles and hanging handles, ads above the windows, black windows with your reflection, flickering fluorescent tubes, the connecting door at the far end.
- routeMap: the lit route map above the doors. The stop names are wrong (Halloway, Ninefield, Last Light, Ash Street), and in Part 3 one of them changes into your name.
- rulesSheet: close-up of the folded lined paper with the handwritten rules.
- phoneScreen: 2:14 AM, no signal, a call coming in (Part 2).
- gangway: the rattling gap between cars, rubber bellows, the door window into the last car (only darkness and one hanging handle swinging).
- platform: a station seen through the doors. Tiled wall with the station name, one flickering light, and someone waiting who shouldn't be there.
- ticket: close-up of a paper ticket with punched holes; June's 1987 ticket, punched so many times it's almost lace.
Lighting: CAR (cold green-white fluorescent, flickering), DARK (lights out: only the phone screen, the route map glow and the platform light), PLATFORM (warm, wrong).

## Outline
1. **Wake up** (written by hand with Rishi; see briefs/part1.json): opens quiet: the train home after a long day, a small nap. "If only you knew how wrong you'd be." You wake at 2:14 to silence, newspapers over every face, black windows, a route map of strange stops. The old woman across the aisle is watching you; her face falls: "Oh no. Not another one." "Where are we?" "I'm not sure, sweetheart. I wish I knew." She sits beside you, introduces herself as June, small talk (long day; you're headed to Elm Street; Elm Street isn't on the map). She gives you the nine rules, read one by one. The conductor comes first: the man in the red tracksuit nearly talks back and June kicks his shoe; your ticket gets its first hole; June's is punched like lace: "One hole for every stop you miss." Then the man in red mocks the passengers; they copy him; June hisses "Freeze"; you do, he doesn't; they all step toward him; the lights go out; his seat is empty; on the floor a newspaper with a man in a red tracksuit on the front page.
2. **The last car** (rework after Part 1): the man in red is already gone (rule nine). Part 2 keeps the newspapers' shared date (decades old), the lights going out (rule three: eyes shut, the punch clicking by your ear), and ends on your phone ringing with no signal: your mom, saying your name (rule five). June stays gentle and honest.
3. **The handwriting**: June takes the phone and hangs up. "Never answer." She tells you about her stop in 1987, and what was waiting on the platform, and how she stayed in her seat. Midway twist: you look at the rules again and notice the handwriting is yours. The loops, the crossed sevens. In the black window your reflection is still reading the page after you've looked up, then it blinks first (rule eight). You look away just in time. You've been here before. Cliffhanger: the map above the doors flickers, and the next stop changes to your name.
4. **Your stop**: the train slows. Through the doors, the platform is your street, and your mom is standing there waving. Rule six says get off no matter what. June grabs your wrist, terrified: "That's what was waiting for me. Please. Don't." You hesitate, and the doors close. As the train pulls away, the woman on the platform stops waving and runs alongside the window, smiling too wide. Midway twist: "Ticket, please." A second hole. Cliffhanger: from the last car, a man's voice calls June's name, softly, the way someone who loved her would. June stands up, and before you can stop her, she walks through the door. A moment later, from the last car, June calls your name.
5. **Don't miss your stop**: you don't follow her (rule four), and you don't answer (rule five). You realize who wrote the list and why: rule seven, "Do not fall asleep again," was a note to yourself. Last time, you got out. The lights die; eyes shut; something cold breathes beside your face; then a warm, shaking hand finds yours and you know it's really June. The doors chime. The map shows your name again. You stand up, and every passenger stands with you (rule nine; you freeze, and they sit). The platform is full of things that want you to stay. You pull June off with you, no matter what's there. You jolt awake. You're back on the normal train you fell asleep on: bright lights, a few tired real people on their phones, an ad for a mattress sale, the speaker announcing your stop by its real name. Your phone says 2:15 AM and has full signal. June's long knitted scarf is in your lap, and the seat beside you is empty. Final image: you take out a pen, write "1. Do not make eye contact with the other passengers," fold the page, and tuck it into the seat pocket for the next person.

## Story rules
- Up to three minutes per part. Let moments breathe: quiet beats ("hold") while you take in a place or a stare lasts too long. Make each character's introduction count, and never name someone before they introduce themselves or are named in the story.
- Open every part on its hook, the strangest thing, in the first sentence.
- Something happens every few seconds. A rule shows up on screen as the handwritten line when it matters, never as a list read out loud. At most one or two rules per part are quoted.
- The characters talk: June is gentle, tired and sorry for you, the man in red is loud, the conductor only ever says "Ticket, please."
- A twist midway through each part, and a cliffhanger at the end. Never reveal who wrote the rules before Part 3, or why before Part 5, but keep every clue fair.
- Times shown on the phone, never read as numbers more than once.
