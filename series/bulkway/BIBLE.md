# Snowed In at Bulkway: story bible

## Premise
A normal blizzard (not a disaster movie) buries a Bulkway Warehouse Club store for 30 days. Three night-shift workers, and one customer who hid inside at closing, have to survive inside a 140,000 square foot warehouse store with no outside help. Second person: the viewer is "you", the night-shift stocker.

## Characters
- **You**: early 20s night stocker. Orange knit beanie (signature), navy store vest over a gray hoodie, NIGHT CREW name tag, box cutter. Practical, notices details. Never named.
- **Dana**: late 50s night manager. Gray bob, glasses on a cord, red fleece, big key ring. Calm, does the math out loud, the one in charge.
- **Marcus**: tall forklift driver. Short beard, yellow hi-vis vest, ear defenders. Few words, strong, solves physical problems.
- **Ray**: the customer who hid in the pet food aisle at closing because he had nowhere to go in the storm. Hood up, 40s, wary. Revealed in Part 2. Not a villain: scared and stockpiling for himself, and in the end his stash saves them.

## Sets (already built, in assets/sets.js)
entrance (front glass doors, BULKWAY sign, snow drift), aisle (racking aisle: stock cans, water, pet or mixed; aisle signs), ladder, bedding aisle and mattresses, frozenWall (freezer doors), freezerDrip (fog, drip, puddle), doorGap, loadingDock (bay door, forklift, wall monitor), monitorScreen, office (desk, 3x3 camera grid, store map), waterWall, wallClock, snow.
UI cards (assets/ui.js): messageBubble, countyAlert, generatorMeter, phoneScreen (time, day), camGrid, camFull, endCard.
Lighting: FULL (warm, all lights) and GEN (generator: cold, every other light off). After the generator dies in Part 2, a third state is needed: DARK (flashlights and lanterns only).

## Outline
1. **The storm** (Night 1): storm hits, generator kicks in, doors buried, 3 days of fuel, the freezers start dripping. Cliffhanger: the security camera shows someone sitting in the pet food aisle who must have been inside since before close.
2. **The guest** (Days 1 to 3): they find Ray. Tense standoff, then he explains he hid at closing because he had nowhere to go. The crew argues about trusting him. Problem: thousands of pounds of frozen food is thawing. Fix: Marcus opens the bay door a foot and they forklift pallets into the snow drift, which works as a freezer. Cliffhanger: the generator dies on day 3, a full day early, and the fuel can by it is empty. Someone drained it.
3. **The cold** (Days 4 to 12): no power, the store drops toward freezing. Fix: they build a small room out of display mattresses and camping tents in the camping aisle, with sleeping bags rated below zero and body heat. Someone wants to light the propane grills from the seasonal aisle indoors, and Dana stops them (carbon monoxide would kill everyone in their sleep). Cliffhanger: you catch Ray at the bay door at night with a bag of supplies.
4. **The roof** (Days 13 to 24): the roof creaks under the snow. A soft spot over the dry-goods section. They climb through the roof hatch on a rope line and shovel a section clear. Ray admits he drained the generator fuel and has been stockpiling so he can walk out alone. Cliffhanger: the roof over aisle fourteen drops a foot with a crack.
5. **Day 30** (Days 25 to 30): the collapse forces everyone into the loading dock area. Ray's stash (fuel, batteries, a hand-crank radio) is what keeps them alive and gets a signal out. A plow convoy reaches the lot on day 30. Final image: the front doors slide fully open for the first time in a month.

## Story rules
- Every line moves the story. Survival detail only appears inside an event (doing something), never as a lecture.
- Two to four real events per part, each with a clear problem and a clever, believable fix.
- At most two numbers on screen per part. Show amounts as pictures (a wall of pallets, a sagging roof, a frosted thermometer) instead of stat cards.
- Each part ends on a cliffhanger that makes the next part necessary. The last part ends the story.
- Plain, concrete narration in second person. No dramatic one-word lines that say nothing.
